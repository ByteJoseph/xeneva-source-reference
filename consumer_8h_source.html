4/*
5 * DCL <linux/gpio/consumer.h> -- the direction enum and the two calls
6 * serial_core.c makes for RS485 transceiver control.
7 *
8 * There is no GPIO controller in Xeneva, and this header is the whole
9 * distance between that fact and a driver that must still compile:
10 *
11 * devm_gpiod_get_optional() returns NULL, which is mainline's own answer
12 * for an *optional* descriptor on a system with
13 * no line behind that name -- the callers at
14 * serial_core.c:3573 and :3583 read it into a
15 * desc and hand it straight to...
16 * gpiod_set_value_cansleep() ...which does nothing. Both are written to
17 * tolerate a NULL desc, because that is the only
18 * value they will ever be given here.
19 *
20 * So the RS485 term and rx-during-tx lines are absent rather than simulated,
21 * which is what an unconnected transceiver-enable pin is: nothing to assert,
22 * nothing to deassert, and the "works fine for short cables" comment at
23 * :3571 becomes "works for the half-duplex case nobody configured".
24 *
25 * The enum is mainline's bit-for-bit (the GPIOD_FLAGS_BIT_* values matter --
26 * `gpiod_get` returns -EPROBE_DEFER based on them upstream, and a wrong
27 * number here would be read back by nothing but is cheap to keep right).
28 *
29 * upstream include/linux/gpio/consumer.h (mainline v7.2)
30 */
