4/*
5 * DCL <asm/io.h> -- port I/O accessors.
6 *
7 * ARM64 has no x86 I/O port space, but 8250's probe and PIO paths carry
8 * inb/outb/inl/outl calls that must still *compile* even though every port we
9 * intend to bind is MMIO (QEMU's pci-serial BAR, iMX8MP's UART -- both
10 * reach registers through readb/writeb, already in <linux/kernel.h>).
11 *
12 * So these are honest failures, not silent ones: a read answers 0 and a write
13 * drops, and the first of either logs once. 0 rather than 0xff on purpose --
14 * an all-ones answer makes LSR look like "transmitter always empty", which
15 * would let the driver believe it is talking to a live UART, while 0 says
16 * "no line is ready" and drives every polling loop into its timeout and out
17 * again. Failing toward the timeout is what keeps a misconfigured port from
18 * becoming a boot hang.
19 *
20 * When a board that genuinely needs PIO shows up, the body becomes a window
21 * (PCI I/O space is a mapping on ARM64, not an x86 instruction) and this
22 * comment is the thing to delete.
23 */
24

33/*
34 * ioremap()/iounmap() and the be32 accessors, reached the way a mainline
35 * driver reaches them: through <asm/io.h>.
36 *
37 * DCL deliberately houses ioremap in <linux/io.h> rather than here (see the
38 * note at the top of that header: on arm64 ioremap is a plain P2V, and it
39 * belongs with the other map/unmap declarations). The consequence was that
40 * nothing included it -- every mainline source says <asm/io.h> and calls
41 * ioremap, so 8250_port.c:2908 compiled as an implicit `int ioremap()` and
42 * assigned the result to `void *port->membase`. On this LLP64 target that is
43 * not a warning to tolerate: the int is 32 bits, membase is 8, and the pointer
44 * would have its top half zeroed before a single register was read through it.
45 * Re-exporting the header here is what makes the declaration reach the
46 * call sites that were always going to ask for it.
47 */
48#include <linux/io.h>
