4/*
5 * DCL <linux/interrupt.h> -- request_irq()/free_irq() over Xeneva's GIC.
6 *
7 * The GIC already keeps a per-SPI callback table (KernelAA64/Hal/gic.c:
8 * GICRegisterSPIHandler / GICCallSPIHandler, typed void (*)(int spi)), so the
9 * adapter is small: record (irq -> handler, dev_id) and install one dispatcher
10 * as that SPI's handler. Bodies land in DCL/linux_irq_shim.c.
11 *
12 * IRQ numbers: this is where a faithful adapter earns its keep. On ARM64 the
13 * GIC numbers SGIs 0-15, PPIs 16-31 and SPIs from 32, and Linux's irq number
14 * on these boards is the HW irq, so the SPI id is irq - 32. A negative result
15 * means somebody asked for an SGI/PPI -- a per-CPU line no serial driver owns
16 * -- and is refused rather than quietly registered on the wrong vector.
17 *
18 * One handler per irq. IRQF_SHARED is accepted because 8250_port.c:2314 sets
19 * it unconditionally for iotype PORT_UNKNOWN, but nothing in this tree shares
20 * an irq yet: a fabricated share list would be the kind of quiet wrongness
21 * that surfaces later as a lost interrupt. If a second driver ever asks, that
22 * is the moment to grow it.
23 *
24 * irqreturn_t and IRQ_NONE/IRQ_HANDLED/IRQ_WAKE_THREAD are already in
25 * <linux/kernel.h>, as are the delay primitives -- see <linux/delay.h>.
26 */
